01 · Orientation

Turning to the south pole

The Moon’s axis is tilted only about 1.5°. At the pole the Sun never climbs more than a few degrees: light arrives sideways, high ground can stay lit for most of the year, and low ground can stay dark for ever.

01 · The site

Connecting Ridge

Within 150 km of the pole the crater rims catch the low Sun while their floors never see it. Report 02 scored four NASA candidate regions; the structures in this report are drawn on Connecting Ridge, between Shackleton and de Gerlache: long sunlit periods, a shadowed floor within reach, and a shoulder flat enough for the pad.

01 · Site data · elevation

LOLA polar elevation

LOLA is the laser altimeter on NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter: it measured the height of the ground, and this layer is that height as colour. Blue is low — the crater floors — and pale is high — the rims. The base site sits on Connecting Ridge, the high crest between Shackleton and de Gerlache, because at the pole height is what buys sunlight. 01 · Site data · cold

Diviner cold traps

Crater floors that never see the Sun stay below 110 K — cold enough to keep water ice for billions of years. The blue marks these cold traps; Shackleton’s floor, at about 90 K, is the one next door. 02 · The block

One hexagon. One number.

Six side faces lean alternately in and out. At 0° the block is a plain prism; at 35.3° its faces are those of a cube on its corner. The tilt is the only thing that changes, and it is what makes the blocks lock.

02 · Robot interface

How the robot holds the block

Report 01 compared end-effectors on the osteomorphic block. The hexagonal tilt block changes the problem: every block has the same flat top, and the tilted side faces alternate, so the top is a truncated triangle with three short edges. Five ways to grip it, on the same blocks, the same four steps: Report 01’s twist-key and flush side handle carried over, and three that use the new geometry.

Why the top face wins

Every block in the family shares the flat top, so one top-gripping effector serves pavement, wall, column and shell. Side faces change tilt from ring to ring; a side jaw would need five settings.

Why the pockets help

Three shallow triangular seats printed at the short top edges, 26 mm deep, with 20 mm of material left to the face. They are the same on all five tilts, so one three-finger hand picks every block in the family.

What stays lab-compatible

Option 2 runs on the lab's C-jaw as it is and needs one free side face. The other four grip from above, so one wrist serves pavement, wall, column and shell; the twist-key slot and the finger pockets are printed into the top, the same on every tilt.

07 · Site notes

Four candidate regions, one chosen for the walk

Connecting Ridge, de Gerlache Rim 2, Malapert Massif and Nobile Rim 1 scored in Report 02 on light, ice distance, slope and landing access. The structures in this report are drawn on Connecting Ridge: long sunlit periods, a shadowed floor within reach, and a flat shoulder for the pad.

Map tool

The site view links to JSMARS, a zero-install planetary GIS in the browser by Prof. Anwar Sounny-Slitine. It is in beta; the Moon basemap and layers are still being added, so the link opens the region rather than a prepared map.

What the site has to give

A flat, compacted shoulder for the pad; an approach below 20° for surface vehicles; a berm line between pad and vault; and foundation seats levelled to under 1° for element placement.

What NASA has not decided

Site preparation. The Moon Base ground rules (Oct 2026) say compaction and levelling are "not yet resolved" and ask bidders to state their assumptions. The pavement in this report is that assumption, made concrete.

08 · Programme

What a settlement needs that a base does not

Analog stations in Antarctica, submarines and the ISS agree on what keeps people well in a sealed place. All of it is shells, seats and ports from the same block; the difference is what goes behind the port.

Body

Greenhouse and food; gym and a long straight run; medical bay with an isolation room; a deep radiation shelter (one thickened vault); dust control with a clean/dirty line through the whole plan.

Mind

Tunable light on a 24-hour cycle; a private cabin each and quiet rooms; Earth-facing windows and varied routes; one room with no function at all.

Community

One table big enough for everyone and a real kitchen; a workshop open to residents; an arts and science institution that doubles as the town hall; private, good links home.

Systems

Water and waste loops with visible reserves; thermal zoning rather than one climate; a bathhouse.

Industry

Block printing and laser forming at the regolith face; a maintenance yard for the robot fleet; logistics bays rated for repeated docking.

Institution

The cultural wing: the same lattice with studios, labs and galleries behind the ports, and the Earth in every window.
